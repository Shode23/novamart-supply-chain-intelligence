# Business Insights

1. Revenue growth is strong, but cost pressure is rising faster

NovaMart’s revenue increased from about ₦757.77M in 2023 to ₦1.706B in 2025, with growth of roughly 49.8% in 2024 and 50.3% in 2025. However, procurement spend increased even faster, rising by about 64.0% in 2024 and 68.2% in 2025. Procurement spend intensity also climbed from 62.36% of revenue in 2023 to 76.41% in 2025.

Business implication: strong top-line growth is being accompanied by heavier procurement cost pressure, which can erode profitability if not controlled.

Recommendation: management should prioritize supplier price negotiations, purchasing efficiency, category-level cost monitoring, and procurement planning so that procurement spend does not continue to outpace revenue growth.

2. Rice and Cooking Oil are major revenue engines — and major concentration risks

Rice contributed about 43.69% of total revenue, while Cooking Oil contributed about 32.12%. Together, they generated roughly 75.8% of revenue. They also account for the majority of current inventory value and procurement spend.

Business implication: these categories are commercially critical, but NovaMart is highly dependent on a narrow portion of its product portfolio. Supply disruption, price volatility, or declining demand in either category could materially affect performance.

Recommendation: protect supply continuity for these categories while gradually strengthening other categories with attractive demand or margin potential. Management should also monitor supplier dependency within Rice and Cooking Oil more closely than lower-value categories.

3. High-spend supplier exposure does not always correspond with strong reliability

NovaMart spent about ₦2.551B on procurement across the analytical period, while overall supplier on-time delivery was only around 52.6%. More importantly, some strategically important suppliers had weak reliability. For example, Supplier03 represented approximately ₦383.56M of procurement spend but delivered only about 29.17% of purchase orders on time.

Business implication: NovaMart may be commercially dependent on suppliers that are not operationally dependable, creating replenishment and service risk.

Recommendation: create a supplier performance scorecard combining spend, OTD, category dependency, lead time, and strategic importance. High-spend/low-OTD suppliers should be prioritized for corrective-action discussions, service-level agreements, or alternative-source development.

4. Inventory availability improved substantially, but imbalances remain

Stock-out rates improved from approximately 0.64% in 2023 to 0.13% in 2025, and no current product-warehouse position is at zero stock. However, 48 positions are at or below reorder level and 46 are at or below safety stock. The dataset also reveals extreme days-of-supply cases for some products, while others have only a few days of inventory remaining.

Business implication: NovaMart has improved availability overall, but the problem has shifted from widespread stock-outs toward inventory imbalance — too much stock in some items and insufficient buffers in others.

Recommendation: move toward product-warehouse-specific replenishment management. Products with very high days of supply should be reviewed for purchasing reduction or redistribution, while items near safety stock should receive priority replenishment based on demand velocity and lead time.

5. Logistics service has improved while transport efficiency has strengthened

On-time delivery increased from 78% in 2023 to 91% in 2025. At the same time, transport cost as a percentage of revenue declined from approximately 22.53% to 17.90%.

Distance is also the dominant observable transport-cost driver, with a correlation of approximately 0.986 between distance and transport cost.

Business implication: NovaMart is delivering stronger customer service while gaining transport-cost efficiency relative to revenue growth. This is one of the clearest areas of operational improvement.

Recommendation: preserve the practices driving OTD improvement, while using route distance, shipment consolidation, warehouse allocation, and delivery-network planning as the main levers for further transport-cost optimization.

6. Port Harcourt currently appears to be the strongest warehouse commercially

Port Harcourt generated approximately ₦1.335B, or 37.1% of total revenue, despite not having the highest number of orders. It also produced the strongest 2025 estimated contribution after transport at approximately ₦27.26M and the highest overall revenue generated per transport naira.

Lagos, by contrast, recorded an estimated negative contribution after transport of approximately ₦2.16M in 2025.

Business implication: revenue alone does not tell the full warehouse-performance story. Warehouse economics differ materially once transport costs are considered.

Recommendation: management should investigate why Port Harcourt converts revenue into post-transport contribution more effectively and whether parts of its customer mix, shipment economics, order profile, or operating practices can be replicated elsewhere. Lagos should receive a focused cost-to-serve review.

7. Cash collection is strong, but operational growth is not translating into equally strong contribution

NovaMart collected approximately 91.66% of recorded revenue, indicating relatively strong cash realization. For 2025, estimated gross margin was around 19.46%, but estimated contribution after transport was only about ₦26.51M, equivalent to roughly 1.55% of revenue.

This figure should remain described as estimated contribution after transport, not operating profit or net profit.

Business implication: the business can generate revenue and collect cash effectively, but procurement and transport costs consume a large portion of the value created.

Recommendation: management should treat margin protection as a cross-functional issue rather than a sales-only objective. Procurement pricing, product mix, transport efficiency, customer economics, and warehouse cost-to-serve should be monitored together.

8. Customer value differs significantly by segment

Wholesalers generated the highest total segment revenue at approximately ₦1.217B, while Retail Chains recorded the highest average order value at about ₦310K. Independent Retailers placed far more orders but with a much lower average order value of around ₦44.7K.

Business implication: customer segments contribute value in different ways. A high-volume small-order segment creates a very different logistics and servicing profile from a lower-volume, high-value segment.

# Top 5 Management Priorities
1. Control procurement cost growth
Procurement spend is growing faster than revenue, and procurement intensity increased from 62.36% in 2023 to 76.41% in 2025. Management should focus on supplier negotiations, purchasing discipline, and tighter category-level cost monitoring.
2. Reduce supplier reliability risk
Overall supplier OTD is only about 52.6%, with some major suppliers performing poorly. High-spend, low-OTD suppliers should be placed under formal performance review, supported by service-level targets and alternative sourcing plans.
3. Improve inventory balance, not just stock availability
Stock-outs have fallen significantly, but NovaMart still has 48 positions at/below reorder level and 46 at/below safety stock, alongside products with excessive days of supply. Replenishment should be managed more precisely at product-warehouse level.
4. Strengthen cost-to-serve management by warehouse and customer segment
Port Harcourt currently shows stronger post-transport economics, while Lagos recorded a slightly negative estimated contribution after transport in 2025. Management should compare route economics, customer mix, shipment characteristics, and service models across warehouses and segments.
5. Protect margin as the business scales
NovaMart is growing and collecting cash effectively, but 2025 estimated contribution after transport remains thin at about ₦26.51M, or 1.55% of revenue. Growth should therefore be managed with equal attention to procurement cost, transport efficiency, product mix, and customer economics.
# Executive takeaway
NovaMart’s next stage of improvement should focus less on generating more volume alone and more on converting growth into stronger, more resilient, and more profitable operations.
