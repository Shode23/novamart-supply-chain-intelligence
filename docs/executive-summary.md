NovaMart Consumer Goods Limited is a national FMCG distributor operating across Lagos, Abuja, and Port Harcourt, serving supermarkets, wholesalers, retail chains, and independent retailers across nine product categories.

As the business expanded, management needed a clearer view of how effectively procurement, inventory, warehouse operations, sales, logistics, and finance were working together to support profitable and reliable customer service. Although the company generated large volumes of operational data, decision-making required a consolidated analytical view of performance across departments.

The NovaMart Supply Chain Intelligence project was developed to transform this operational data into a structured business intelligence solution. The analysis focused on sales growth, product and customer performance, inventory efficiency, supplier reliability, procurement costs, logistics service levels, cash collection, and broader operational efficiency.

The solution combined a relational MySQL database, modular SQL analysis, a governed KPI framework, and an interactive seven-page Power BI dashboard. The final report provides executives and operational teams with a single view of business performance, highlights areas of risk and inefficiency, and supports more informed decisions across the supply chain.

NovaMart experienced strong revenue expansion between 2023 and 2025, but growth also introduced new operational pressures. Procurement spending increased faster than revenue, supplier reliability varied significantly, and inventory was heavily concentrated in a small number of product categories. At the same time, logistics performance improved substantially, with on-time delivery rising from 78% in 2023 to 91% in 2025.

The analysis also showed that revenue concentration in Rice and Cooking Oil created both commercial strength and dependency risk. Supplier performance was uneven, with some high-spend suppliers recording weak on-time delivery. Inventory efficiency improved overall, but low-stock positions and extreme days-of-supply cases revealed opportunities for better replenishment control.

Financially, NovaMart maintained a strong cash collection rate of about 91.66%, while transport cost as a percentage of revenue declined over time. However, procurement intensity increased, and 2025 estimated contribution after transport remained relatively thin, showing that revenue growth did not automatically translate into equally strong operating contribution.

These findings shaped the final dashboard design, which allows users to move from executive performance indicators into detailed analysis of sales, customers, inventory, suppliers, logistics, and financial efficiency.
